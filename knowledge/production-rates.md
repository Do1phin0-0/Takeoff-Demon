# Production Rates for Should-Cost

Industry-average labor-hours per unit. Used by `/should-cost` and `/compare-sub` to build a defensible labor benchmark. **These are averages — tune to your local market once you have data from past jobs.** Override the loaded labor rate with `/override-laborrate`.

**Default loaded labor rates:**
- Residential trades: $65/hr (includes burden)
- Commercial trades: $75/hr (commercial union/non-union mix, higher safety/OSHA cost)
- Civil/sitework operators: $85/hr (operator + machine cost share)
- Structural steel ironworkers: $95/hr (specialty, often union)

Three sections: **RESIDENTIAL**, **COMMERCIAL**, **CIVIL/SITEWORK**.

Format: `LH = labor-hours per crew member`. Productivity assumes a competent crew on a clean, accessible site.

## How to Use in /should-cost

1. Take quantity from takeoff.
2. Multiply by labor-hours-per-unit from this list (use the middle of the range).
3. Multiply by the loaded labor rate matching the trade type (residential / commercial / civil / steel).
4. Add material cost (from price-list.md).
5. Compare to sub bid in `/compare-sub`.

**Example 1 — Residential drywall hang for 80 sheets:**
- 80 sheets ÷ 5 sheets/LH = 16 LH
- 16 LH × $65/hr = $1,040 labor
- Materials (80 × $15.50) = $1,240
- Should-cost subtotal: $2,280

**Example 2 — Commercial structural steel, 25 tons:**
- 25 tons × 11 LH/ton (mid of range) = 275 LH
- 275 LH × $95/hr (steel ironworker) = $26,125 erection labor
- Material: 25 tons × 2,000 lb × $2.40/lb material only = $120,000
- All-in (using the $4.50/lb erected price): 25 × 2,000 × $4.50 = $225,000
- Use the all-in number for the should-cost; the breakdown above just shows reasoning.

**Example 3 — Civil RCP 18" storm pipe, 400 lf, average 8 ft depth:**
- 400 lf ÷ 45 lf/hr (mid) = 8.9 hr crew time
- Crew (3 + machine): 8.9 × 3 × $85/hr = $2,270 labor (rough)
- Material: 400 × $68/lf installed price already includes labor — use the installed price.
- Should-cost: 400 × $68 = $27,200 installed
- Add: bedding stone, manholes (separately), trench shoring if depth >5 ft.
